Wear Before Capacity: Lifetime-Budgeted Prefix KV Caching on High Bandwidth Flash

Submitted to IEEE Computer Architecture Letters on 24 October 2026 · under review

Abstract

Most High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) studies of the key-value (KV) cache of large language models assume single-level cells rated for 100,000 program/erase cycles. We ask what a five-year life allows a prefix KV cache on denser cells, on Mooncake and Qwen-Bailian production traces. At its life budget a region keeps each block for a time that does not depend on its capacity, so writable bytes decide what the cache can do, and cell mode moves GPU-time savings as much as the admission policy does. On 8 stacks, three- and four-bit cells can write only 1.7–3.9% of the bytes HBM evicts for Llama-3.1-70B and 8–18% for DeepSeek-V3’s latent KV.

We then price wear: a block is written only when its expected recomputation savings exceed the price of its wear, and wear stays provably at most a constant ahead of the life schedule. At a 10% budget the price keeps 35.4% and 32.1% of the GPU time that writing every block saves, 5.0–6.0 points more than a lifetime quota given the load curve, at equal bytes. Its gain comes from pacing writes to the load: without a load curve it keeps 0.3–1.2 points more than a budget-tracking frequency rule given one. A NAND-write counter keeps wear on budget under unknown write amplification.
